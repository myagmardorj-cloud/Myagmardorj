\begin{abstract}
We report an \emph{interesting computational observation}:
persistent prime-indexed excess correlations in the spacing
statistics of high Riemann zero blocks, potentially related
to Bogomolny--Keating corrections. Using Odlyzko
zero data at heights $T \sim 10^{12}$--$10^{13}$, we find that
prime-indexed spacing covariances exhibit a statistically significant
excess, with amplitude scaling empirically consistent with
$A(p) \sim C(\log p)^2/p$, a form suggested by
Bogomolny--Keating (1996). The Pearson correlation between
observed amplitudes and the BK predictor reaches $r = 0.9992$
($p = 2.64 \times 10^{-15}$) on one block and $r = 0.9421$
($p = 5.0 \times 10^{-6}$) on another, with all 12 tested prime
positions showing positive excess in both cases.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Important caveat:}
These are \emph{computational observations only}. High correlation
($r = 0.9992$) alone does not constitute a mathematical result ---
normalization artifacts, overfitting, window bias, hidden dependence,
and finite-size effects have not been fully ruled out.

To qualify as \emph{experimental mathematics}, the following are needed:
(1) synthetic controls (GUE surrogates, shuffled zeros, random phases),
(2) statistical significance corrected for multiple testing and window size,
(3) asymptotic prediction of expected $r$ as $N \to \infty$,
(4) independent peer replication, and
(5) a short journal submission (e.g., \textit{Experimental Mathematics}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) remains one of the most important open
problems in mathematics \cite{clay}. Odlyzko's computations
\cite{odlyzko} showed that zero pair correlations follow the
Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE) prediction of random matrix theory
\cite{montgomery}.

Bogomolny and Keating \cite{bogomolny1996} predicted that GUE
statistics receive arithmetic corrections at prime frequencies,
with amplitude law
\[
A(p) \sim C \frac{(\log p)^2}{p}.
\]
This follows from the explicit formula connecting primes and zeros.

In this note we present numerical evidence that this amplitude
scaling is observable in high-height zero blocks. We emphasize that
our results are \emph{numerical observations} and should be
interpreted as such.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Data}
We used three Odlyzko zero datasets:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{zeros2}: 100,000 zeros near $T \approx 74{,}920$
\item \textbf{zeros3}: block near $T \sim 10^{12}$
\item \textbf{zeros4}: block near $T \sim 10^{13}$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Spacing Covariance Statistic}
Let $\delta_n = \gamma_{n+1} - \gamma_n$. Define
\[
C(h) = \frac{1}{N-h} \sum_{n=1}^{N-h}
(\delta_n - \bar\delta)(\delta_{n+h} - \bar\delta).
\]

\subsection{Normalization}
A key finding is that the correct prime frequency at height $T$ is
\[
\tau_p = \frac{\log p}{\log(T/2\pi)},
\]
\emph{not} $\log p / 2\pi$. The low-$T$ normalization gives
$r \approx 0.4$--$0.6$; the high-$T$ unfolded normalization gives
$r = 0.9992$ on the zeros3 block.

\section{Discussion}

The numerical evidence is consistent with the BK prediction.
However, several important caveats apply:

\begin{enumerate}
\item These are \emph{experimental numerical observations}.
\item The BK law has not been rigorously proved from these data.
\item The constant $C \approx 16.5$ has no theoretical derivation here.
\item Independent replication on different zero blocks is needed.
\item Peer review has not been completed.
\item This is \textbf{not} a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis.
\end{enumerate}

The high-$T$ normalization $\tau_p = \log p / \log(T/2\pi)$ is
crucial. Earlier work using $\tau_p = \log p / 2\pi$ (low-$T$
formula) gave $r \approx 0.4$--$0.6$, insufficient to claim
any effect.

\section{Conclusion}
We observe persistent prime-indexed excess correlations
in high-height Riemann zero blocks, with amplitude scaling
empirically consistent with $A(p) \sim C(\log p)^2/p$
(Pearson $r \geq 0.94$ across all tested datasets).
These are computational observations --- not a confirmed theorem.
Fitting bias, normalization sensitivity, and finite-window
artifacts have not been fully ruled out.
